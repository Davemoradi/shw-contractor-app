The difference

A lead is a maybe. A work order is a job.

You already know how the lead business works, because you've paid for it.

Buying a lead
  1. You pay for the lead before you know if it's real
  2. The same lead is commonly sold to several contractors
  3. You drive out and give a free estimate
  4. You get compared on price against three other bids
  5. You win maybe one, and paid for all of them
  6. Then you invoice the homeowner and wait
Taking an SHW work order
  1. The homeowner already filed a claim and has coverage
  2. The work order is assigned to you, not auctioned
  3. You go out to do the work, not to bid on it
  4. No competing quotes — you're the contractor
  5. The homeowner pays you their deductible at the visit — approved or denied
  6. SHW pays the balance on approved invoices, so you're not billing the homeowner

And when a claim gets denied, you're already standing in the kitchen with the diagnosis in hand. That work is yours to quote directly — SHW takes no cut and makes no claim on it.

No surprises

Exactly how you get paid

Most networks make you sign before they'll tell you. Here it is up front, straight out of the agreement you'll sign.

WhatHow it works
Service call feePaid by SHW for your travel to the home and diagnosing the failure. You set your rate on the application; we agree it before you're activated.
LaborYour hourly rate × labor hours, quoted in quarter-hour increments. Travel and diagnosis time are covered by the service call fee rather than billed as labor hours.
Parts mark-upTiered, and capped: 50% under $25, 40% to $50, 30% to $100, 25% to $150, 10% above $150.
DeductibleThe homeowner pays you $75–$150 at the visit, set by their plan. Under their contract it's owed whether the claim is approved or denied — so you're not eating a wasted trip. It's credited against that invoice rather than paid on top of it, meaning it's the first part of your money, in hand the same day.
Who pays the restSHW pays the balance directly on approved invoices. You are not billing or chasing the homeowner for covered work.
WhenNet 30 after invoice approval. Net 15 on Premium.
Non-covered workIf the claim is denied or the homeowner wants more done, that job is yours. You quote it, you bill it, you keep all of it.
Your warrantyYou stand behind repairs for 60 days against normal wear and tear.

Rates are the ones you propose on your application and agree with us before activation. Full terms are in the Master Services Agreement, which you'll read and sign as part of the application — nothing here is hidden until after you pay.
